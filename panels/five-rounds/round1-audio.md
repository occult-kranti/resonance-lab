# Five-panel sequence — round 1, physics and mathematics pedagogy

Role: AI physics/mathematics pedagogy reviewer. Baseline inspected: eleven normalized blueprint records in `public/data/blueprints.json`, their three Newton setup families and eight Atlas views, imported R1–R3 contracts, `blueprint-data.ts`, `BlueprintLibrary.tsx`, `DeviceBench.tsx`, and `RoomListening.tsx`. Findings below derive from source/diagram inspection, not participant research. No feature implementation occurred before the architecture assignment.

## Three prioritized gaps

| Gap | Concrete evidence | Consequence | Proposed decision and observable acceptance |
|---|---|---|---|
| One general glossary does not cover each domain or unit context. | The ten rows of `BLUEPRINT_GLOSSARY` are displayed unchanged for all eleven records. Atlas magnetic SVGs contain `N = mg − Fm`, `F = ½ I² dL/dz`, `ż`, `g`, and millinewtons. Sound plans use CH1/CH2, pilot, pre-roll, drift and cross-talk. Prism geometry uses mm, degrees and nm; sound drawings use cm, despite the normalized parts-table caption referring to metres. | A reader cannot reliably identify force versus frequency, spatial axes versus impedance, angular convention, or source drawing units. A single bare-symbol definition risks teaching a wrong interpretation. | Create domain/context-scoped terminology and explicit per-record term references. Each of the eleven records receives relevant definitions with units and drawing-vs-model context; retain original diagram units and expose their normalized conversion. Acceptance: all mapped term ids resolve; magnetic force/work, sound, optics, coupled-coil and witness records expose their own terminology. |
| Mathematical notation is exposed as raw contract text without a complete interpretation layer. | Imported R1 uses `i` both for current-vector notation and the imaginary unit in impedance expressions. R2 uses `C² = R⁴`, `Re`, `Im`, hats, δ, ε, conjugation, Jacobian rank/nullity and deterministic disks. R3 introduces complex determinant `1 − a beta` and kernel witnesses. The existing glossary only groups `q / i / v`, `a / beta` and a few circuit quantities. | The same symbol can mean different things, and an unexplained inverse-problem term can look like a physical detection result. | Define scoped entries for imaginary unit versus instantaneous current, complex/real fields versus capacitance/resistance, coherent measurement, real dimension, rank, nullity, kernel, determinant, local Jacobian, conjugate, absolute-value magnitude, estimate/hat, error bound and deterministic disk. Acceptance: separate entries preserve collisions; source contracts remain unchanged; no automatic symbol replacement edits their formulas. |
| Amplitude/power and digital/acoustic conventions need explicit equations alongside the existing warnings. | BlueprintLibrary correctly warns that upstream phasors are peak amplitude while the host circuit is RMS, but supplies no immediately usable conversion equation. DeviceBench already closes a finite-time work/storage/loss budget and disclaims voltage gain as energy gain. RoomListening discloses a pressure reflection coefficient, direct `t=d/c`, common listening scale and no RT60, but terms/units are scattered among prose and receipt fields. | A reader could transfer a peak value into an RMS power formula, or treat pressure reflectivity/dBFS/L1 as an energy fraction or calibrated SPL. | Add explicit convention cards: for a sinusoid `Vpeak = √2 Vrms`, average real power conventions, finite-time energy identity, pressure-amplitude versus energy reflectivity, delay/distance/speed units, sample peak dBFS versus Pa/dB SPL, and what RT60/L1 mean and do not mean. Acceptance: worked 1 V peak versus 1 V RMS power example, dimensionally correct units, and no new free-energy/physical calibration claim. |

## Suggested owned deliverable after decision

A small `terminology.ts` module can own stable ids, domains, symbols/aliases, plain-language definitions, units, context restrictions and collision notes, plus explicit blueprint-record and host-model mappings. Equation cards should carry a named amplitude convention and relevant term ids. A validator should check mapped records and references, while identifying raw upstream text as a source layer rather than claiming every possible token has been parsed or scientifically verified.

The existing energy-accounting and scientific boundaries are useful and should be retained. This baseline recommends an interpretation layer, not rewriting numerical engines or source contracts. Later rounds must inspect the resulting artifact, including whether the chosen mapping actually reaches readers and retains correct definitions in exports.
