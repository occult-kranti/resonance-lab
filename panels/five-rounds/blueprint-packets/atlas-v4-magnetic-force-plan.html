Contact and magnetic force

A magnet can change how strongly an object presses on a balance.

N is upward contact force; Fm is the stipulated upward magnetic force and mg the downward weight.

Units: newton (N)

  • A smaller contact reading does not establish changed gravity; N the variable differs from N the unit.

Mass and gravitational acceleration

Mass and gravitational acceleration together determine weight.

In the static comparison g is stipulated as 9.80665 m/s², not inferred from a balance change.

Units: kilogram (kg); metre per second squared (m/s²)

  • m as mass differs from m as the metre unit.

Equations and conventions

Balance contact differs from gravity

N = mg − Fm, only while N ≥ 0

An upward magnetic force can reduce the balance contact force without changing gravity.

Units: N and Fm newtons; m kilograms; g m/s². · Convention: Static supported contact; external magnet reaction carried outside the balance.

  • The contact model does not apply after separation.

Magnetic force changes contact force

Proposed ordinary-force control, no physical measurements. The permanent-magnet arrangement is a qualitative illustration, separate from the R4 hypothetical variable-inductance model. Goal: Explain how an ordinary magnetic force can change a balance’s contact force without changing gravity.

Follow the signal

  1. The object has weight from gravity and may also experience magnetic force.
  2. In the stated static contact configuration, the balance supports the remaining normal force.
  3. Check whether the magnet also acts on the instrument or an external support.

What the source defines

  • This is a qualitative permanent-magnet control proposal, separate from the variable-inductance energy model.
  • The source states the static contact balance N = mg minus the declared upward magnetic force.
  • Original source status: proposed_unbuilt.

What remains unknown

  • Actual field, force, material properties and instrument magnetic susceptibility are not measured.
  • A changed balance display does not establish changed gravitational acceleration.

Measurement checklist

  • Record a no-magnet reference and vary magnet/instrument position without changing the declared support geometry.
  • Track the external reaction and possible direct magnetic response of the instrument.

Questions before a physical experiment

  • Does the balance display represent contact force or total weight?
  • Could the balance itself respond to the magnet?
  • Are mass m and a length measured in metres being confused?
