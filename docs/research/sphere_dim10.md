# Sphere Dimension 10 — Prior Claims & Tests of Location-Dependent / Cosmically-Coupled Physical Constants and Resonances

**Research dimension:** Searches for spatial variation of fundamental constants; tests of Lorentz/position invariance with cavities, clocks, and resonators; GPS/relativity position effects on clocks; and published tests of "object/matter remembers its location" claims — in the context of the LOC-1 claim (channeled "Bashar" material) that **position is an "intrinsic vibrational variable" with effects claimed above 230 kHz**.

**Bottom line:** Mainstream physics has probed position-dependence and orientation-dependence of physical law at precision levels that are essentially "cosmic-coupling detectors": atomic-clock comparisons constrain any drift of the fine-structure constant to ~10⁻¹⁸/yr, coupling of constants to gravitational potential to ~10⁻⁷ or better, modern Michelson–Morley-type cavity experiments constrain anisotropy of light propagation to ~10⁻¹⁸, and trapped-ion clock experiments bound electron-sector Lorentz violation to ~10⁻²¹. All null. Claims that an object "remembers" its location or history (water memory, morphic resonance) have been directly tested and failed replication under blinded conditions.

Search count: 16 distinct queries (7 rounds, some returning 20+ sources). Dates below are publication/access dates as reported by the search index.

## A. Spatial / temporal variation of the fine-structure constant (α)

### 1. Webb/King et al. — the claimed "spatial dipole" of α (contested positive claim)
- **Claim:** Analysis of Keck + VLT quasar absorption spectra suggested α varies directionally across the sky (a spatial "dipole").
- **Source:** King et al., "Spatial variation in the fine-structure constant," MNRAS 422, 3370 (2012); Webb et al., PRL 107, 191101 (2011) [^1^]
- **URL:** https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/422/4/3370/1052357 ; reference list corroboration: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3826645/ ("Webb J. K. et al. Indications of a spatial variation of the fine structure constant. Phys. Rev. Lett. 107, 191101 (2011)")
- **Date:** 2011–2012
- **Excerpt (verbatim):** "Webb et al. (2011) analysed the absorption spectra of distant quasars and found evidence that the fine-structure constant α varies directionally across the sky" (ResearchGate summary of the 2011 PRL).
- **Context:** This is the strongest *positive* claim of spatially varying physics, at the ~10⁻⁵–10⁻⁶ level over gigaparsec scales — and it is explicitly contested. A review notes "quasar absorption spectra provide potential evidence for spatial variation of the fine-structure constant α. These data suggest that the Earth may be moving in the direction of increasing α" while later analyses find the data "compatible with the null result." Dent (arXiv:0805.0318) calls the claims "controversial … and await improvements in data and analysis."
- **Confidence:** High that the claim exists and is contested; low that the dipole is real (multiple re-analyses null).

### 2. Tightest single-quasar bound — no variation
- **Claim:** Δα/α = (−0.155 ± 0.728) × 10⁻⁶ from quasar J110325-264515 (z = 1.8389) — consistent with zero.
- **Source:** Le (2020), "Stringent limit on space-time variation of fine-structure constant," PMC [^2^]
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7511822/
- **Date:** 2020
- **Excerpt (verbatim):** "The obtained parameter is Δα/α = (−0.155±0.728)×10⁻⁶… We determine a strong limit of Δα/α = (−0.155±0.728)×10⁻⁶, which consists of dipole variation."
- **Context:** Typical modern single-absorber constraints sit at ~10⁻⁶; older Keck ensemble analyses claimed (−5.7±1.1)×10⁻⁵.
- **Confidence:** High (peer-reviewed; consistent with the null-consensus literature).

### 3. JWST high-redshift null results
- **Claim:** Δα/α consistent with zero at z = 2.5–9.5; combined Δα/α = (0.2 ± 0.7) × 10⁻⁴; four sky-direction subsamples also null at ~2×10⁻⁴.
- **Source:** Jiang, Fu, Wang & Wang, [O III] emission-line constraints (quoted in arXiv search results) [^3^]
- **URL:** (referenced within) https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2016185… (search excerpt); primary listing via arXiv
- **Date:** 2024–2025 (per search index)
- **Excerpt (verbatim):** "The calculated Δα/α values are consistent with zero within 1σ at all redshifts… We further test the spatial variation in α using four subsamples of galaxies in four different directions on the sky. The measured Δα/α values are consistent with zero at a 1σ level of ∼2 × 10⁻⁴."
- **Context:** Independent tracer (emission rather than absorption) — no spatial variation seen.
- **Confidence:** High.

### 4. Oklo natural reactor — 2-billion-year null test
- **Claim:** The Oklo natural fission reactor (Gabon, ~1.8–2 Gyr ago) bounds the change of α to ~10⁻⁷–10⁻⁸.
- **Source:** Damour & Dyson, "The Oklo bound on the time variation of the fine-structure constant," Nucl. Phys. B480 (1996); Petrov et al. (2006) [^4^]
- **URL:** https://repo-archives.ihes.fr/FONDS_IHES/I_Prepublications/DAMOUR/1994-1998/P_96_40/P_96_40.pdf ; https://atomfizika.elte.hu/akos/orak/modfizszem/cikkek/oklo2.pdf
- **Date:** 1996 / 2005–2006
- **Excerpt (verbatim):** "Our final (95% C.L.) results are: −0.9×10⁻⁷ < (α_Oklo − α_now)/α < 1.2×10⁻⁷ and −6.7×10⁻¹⁷ yr⁻¹ < α̇_averaged/α < 5.0×10⁻¹⁷ yr⁻¹" (Damour & Dyson 1996). Petrov et al.: "Contrary to results of some previous papers we find no evidence for the change of the fine structure constant in the past and obtain new, most accurate limits on its variation with time: −4·10⁻¹⁷ year⁻¹ ≤ α̇/α ≤ 3·10⁻¹⁷ year⁻¹."
- **Context:** A "geological" position/epoch test: nuclear physics 2 Gyr ago at a different gravitational environment was the same to ~1 part in 10⁷–10⁸.
- **Confidence:** High (standard reference bound; some model dependence in temperature corrections).

### 5. Laboratory clock drift limits on α — 10⁻¹⁷ to 10⁻¹⁸ per year
- **Claim:** Rosenband et al. (2008, Science): α̇/α = (−1.6 ± 2.3) × 10⁻¹⁷/yr from Al⁺/Hg⁺ ratio. Lange et al. (2021, PRL): improved to 1.0(1.1) × 10⁻¹⁸/yr.
- **Source:** PubMed abstract; arXiv abstract of Lange et al. [^5^]
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18323415/ ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06620
- **Date:** 2008; 2021 (arXiv 2020-10-13)
- **Excerpt (verbatim):** "Repeated measurements during the past year yield a preliminary constraint on the temporal variation of the fine-structure constant alpha of alpha/alpha = (−1.6+/−2.3) x 10(−17)/year" (Rosenband). "We improve by factors of about 20 and 2 the limits for fractional temporal variations of the fine structure constant α to 1.0(1.1)×10⁻¹⁸/yr and of the proton-to-electron mass ratio μ to −8(36)×10⁻¹⁸/yr" (Lange).
- **Context:** Any "vibrational variable" that changed atomic structure over a year would have shown up at 18 decimal places. Nothing did.
- **Confidence:** Very high (flagship NIST/PTB results).

### 6. Dysprosium spectroscopy — direct test of position/potential coupling of α
- **Claim:** Leefer et al. (2013): α̇/α = (−5.8 ± 6.9) × 10⁻¹⁷/yr and, crucially for LOC-1, coupling of α to gravitational potential k_α = (−5.5 ± 5.2) × 10⁻⁷ — null.
- **Source:** Leefer, Weber, Cingöz, Torgerson, Budker, PRL 111, 060801 [^6^]
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.6940 ; https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevLett.111.060801
- **Date:** 2013-04-25 (arXiv) / Aug 2013 (PRL)
- **Excerpt (verbatim):** "Linear variation of α is found to be α̇/α = (−5.8±6.9)×10⁻¹⁷ yr⁻¹, consistent with zero. The same data are used to constrain the dimensionless parameter k_α, characterizing a possible coupling of α to a changing gravitational potential. We find that k_α = (−5.5±5.2)×10⁻⁷, essentially consistent with zero and the best constraint to date."
- **Context:** As Earth orbits, the solar potential at the lab changes; a "position-dependent" α would modulate Dy transition frequencies annually. Nothing seen at ~5×10⁻⁷.
- **Confidence:** Very high.

### 7. Blatt et al. (2008) — Sr clocks vs. gravity coupling, all null

- **Context:** Combines Boulder/Paris/Tokyo Sr clock data with Hg⁺ and H-maser records.

- **Context:** This is exactly the LOC-1 question posed quantitatively: does local physics depend on position in a cosmic field? Answer: not at ~10⁻⁸–10⁻⁹ per unit c²-potential.
- **Confidence:** Very high.

### 9. Dent (2008) — Eötvös tests bound potential-correlated variation at 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁵

## B. Lorentz invariance / orientation-of-lab tests (Michelson–Morley family)

- **Claim:** Anisotropy of c: Δc/c = (2.6 ± 1.7) × 10⁻¹⁵; RMS parameter β−δ−1/2 = (−2.2 ± 1.5) × 10⁻⁹; SME limits to 10⁻¹⁵.

- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12906465/

- **Context:** The cavity resonance frequencies were monitored as Earth rotated for ~1 yr — i.e., continuous scanning of orientation in cosmic frames. Null.

## C. Position-dependent clock rates (GPS, redshift, geodesy) — position matters *only* via gravity, exactly as GR predicts

### 22. GPS relativistic corrections — position dependence of clock rate is real but fully accounted
- **Claim:** GPS satellite clocks gain ~38 µs/day (net) vs. ground; +45 µs/day gravitational, −7 µs/day velocity; satellite clocks are pre-offset by −4.4645×10⁻¹⁰ (10.22999999543 MHz).
- **Source:** NIST Technical Note 1385; Ashby, "Relativity in the Global Positioning System" (Living Rev. / PMC) [^22^]
- **URL:** https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/TN/nbstechnicalnote1385.pdf ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5253894/
- **Date:** (NIST TN, archival); Living Rev. 2002-12-20
- **Excerpt (verbatim):** "To compensate for the relativistic effects… atomic clocks in GPS satellites are given a fixed fractional frequency offset of −4.4645 × 10⁻¹⁰." And: "Neglect of these effects in satellite navigation systems would result in position errors on the order of tens of meters."
- **Context:** Position (gravitational potential) demonstrably changes clock rates — but *exactly* by ΔU/c², with zero anomalous residual. This is the real, mundane "position is a variable" effect.
- **Confidence:** Very high (engineering fact).

## Overall assessment for LOC-1
- **Observed fact:** Position does affect clocks — solely through the gravitational potential (ΔU/c²), verified from 10,000 km (GP-A) down to 1 mm (JILA 2022) and across 457 km (PTB–MPQ 2024), with residuals at the measurement noise floor.
- **Observed fact:** Orientation/velocity through space (the modern "aether wind" question) is null to 10⁻¹⁸–10⁻¹⁹ (photon) and ~10⁻²¹ (electron).
- **Observed fact:** Fundamental constants do not vary with position in the gravitational field to ~10⁻⁷–10⁻⁹ coupling precision, nor drift in time beyond ~10⁻¹⁸/yr.
- **Observed fact:** "Matter remembers location/history" claims (water memory, morphic resonance) were tested and failed blinded replication.
- **Inference:** Any LOC-1 effect above 230 kHz that couples to frequency, energy, or motion is excluded by orders of magnitude; if it couples to nothing measurable, it is outside testable science. **Overall confidence: High.**
